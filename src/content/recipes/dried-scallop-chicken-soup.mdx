<ol class="steps">

<Step id="crush_soak" title="Crush and soak">
  Break the <Ref of="scallops" /> into pieces with pliers — crushing first is
  dad's trick to shorten the soak — then rinse them and cover with hot water.
  A minute in the microwave, covered, and about ten minutes' rest gets them
  soft.
</Step>

<Step id="cut_chicken" title="Cut the chicken">
  Trim the <Ref of="chicken" /> and cut it into slices, then stack the slices
  and cut into strips. Partially frozen meat cuts much more cleanly.
</Step>

<Step id="marinate" title="Velvet the chicken">
  Mix the <Ref of="chicken" /> with the <Ref of="marinade" /> — the water is
  about two tablespoons — stirring well past "combined": the marinade should
  disappear into the meat with no lumps left.
</Step>

<Step id="shred" title="Shred the scallops">
  Drain the soaked <Ref of="scallops" />, squeezing them out over the bowl —
  save every drop of the soaking water; it's flavor you'll pour back in —
  then shred them by hand or with a spoon until no big pieces remain.
</Step>

<Step id="toast" title="Toast the scallops">
  Heat the <Ref of="frying_oil" /> in the soup pot over gentle heat and
  stir-fry the shredded <Ref of="scallops" /> for 40–50 seconds, keeping them
  moving so they don't stick. This dry toasting wakes an aroma the soak alone
  can't.
</Step>

<Step id="build_broth" title="Build the broth">
  Pour in the saved soaking water, the <Ref of="broth" fill="chicken_broth" />,
  and 6–8 cups (about 1.5–2 L) of hot <Ref of="broth" fill="water" /> —
  pre-boiled water saves time. Bring it all to a boil.
</Step>

<Step id="steep" title="Let it steep">
  Lid on, heat off: let the pot rest five minutes. The <Ref of="scallops" />
  release the rest of their flavor into the broth as it sits.
</Step>

<Step id="mix_slurry" title="Mix the slurry">
  Stir the <Ref of="thickener" fill="cornstarch" /> into about 5 tablespoons
  of cold <Ref of="thickener" fill="water" /> until smooth. Never add it dry
  to hot soup — it seizes into one big lump; the slurry step is
  non-negotiable.
</Step>

<Step id="thicken" title="Thicken off the boil">
  Drop the heat as low as it goes — or off entirely. Pour the slurry in
  slowly while stirring along the bottom of the pot so nothing settles.
  Check as you go: thin drinks like a broth, thick coats the spoon. Not
  where you want it? Mix more slurry — it's your call.
</Step>

<Step id="season" title="Season">
  Turn the heat back on and add the <Ref of="seasonings" /> and the
  <Ref of="salinity" />, stirring and tasting between additions — stop short
  of "too salty".
</Step>

<Step id="color" title="Color it amber">
  The <Ref of="seasonings" fill="dark_soy_sauce" /> is for the eye more than
  the tongue: add a little, stir, look. Stop when the soup reads amber —
  banquet shark fin soup got its color from exactly this bottle.
</Step>

<Step id="coat" title="Oil the chicken">
  Just before the chicken goes in, stir the <Ref of="coating_oil" /> through
  it — oiled strips slide apart in the pot instead of clumping.
</Step>

<Step id="add_chicken" title="Add the chicken">
  Drop to low heat — high heat sets the strips into clumps before you can
  separate them. Add the <Ref of="chicken" /> gradually, nudging the pieces
  apart as they land, and give it 40–50 seconds to cook through.
</Step>

<Step id="add_crab" title="The fancy version" when="has(upgrade, 'crab_meat')">
  Fold in the <Ref of="upgrade" /> with the chicken so it just warms
  through — this is the "really, really fancy" banquet version.
</Step>

<Step id="finish" title="Finish">
  Off the boil, stir in the <Ref of="finishers" /> — sesame aroma fades if
  it's boiled, the sugar rounds off the salt, and white pepper is the quiet
  heat under every Cantonese thick soup. Taste once more and serve.
</Step>

</ol>

### Tips

- The luxury was never the fin — it was the broth and the texture. That's why
  this soup can replace shark fin soup outright: everything people actually
  tasted is still here, and the umami is better.
- Most of the sodium is structural — dried seafood, fish sauce, broth. If
  you're watching salt, cut the added salt and season bowl by bowl; the
  scallop umami carries more than you'd think.
- Dried scallops have no real expiry date. Keep the box in the freezer and
  this becomes a pantry soup: everything else is supermarket-ordinary.
- Restaurant versions add more seafood — real crab is dad's pick. Mushrooms,
  by his ruling, don't belong in this one.
