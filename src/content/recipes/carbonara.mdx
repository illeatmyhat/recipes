<ol class="steps">

<Step id="boil" title="Boil the water">
  Bring a large pot of water to a boil and salt it with most of the
  <Ref of="seasoning" fill="salt" /> — the water should taste pleasantly of
  the sea, knowing almost all of that salt drains away.
</Step>

<Step id="render" title="Render the guanciale">
  Cut the <Ref of="pork" /> into thin strips. Warm the
  <Ref of="aromatics" /> in a wide pan over medium heat — the
  <Ref of="aromatics" fill="garlic" /> goes in crushed and whole — then add
  the strips and let the fat render slowly until they brown. Fish the garlic
  out once it has colored: its perfume stays in the fat, the clove itself
  was never going to be eaten.
</Step>

<Step id="sauce" title="Beat the sauce">
  In a bowl big enough to hold the finished dish, beat the
  <Ref of="eggs" /> with the finely grated <Ref of="cheese" /> and a small
  pinch of the <Ref of="seasoning" fill="salt" /> until no streaks remain.
  This bowl is where the carbonara will actually be made — off any flame.
</Step>

<Step id="cook_pasta" title="Cook the spaghetti">
  Drop the <Ref of="pasta" /> into the boiling water and cook it al dente.
  Before draining, save a cup of the cooking water — cloudy with starch,
  it's the tool that loosens and binds the sauce.
</Step>

<Step id="insurance_rest" title="Insurance: let the heat fade" when="insurance">
  Kill the flame under the pan and let it sit two or three minutes while
  the pasta finishes. Clumped cheese is a temperature wound — you don't
  need a thermometer, you need a pause. As they say in Italy: just turn
  off the heat, it's done.
</Step>

<Step id="combine" title="Marry pasta and fat">
  Drain the spaghetti and tumble it into the pan with the <Ref of="pork" />
  and its fat, heat OFF, tossing until every strand is glossy.
</Step>

<Step id="mantecare" title="Make the silk">
  Now move everything into the bowl with the egg mixture and stir fast.
  Residual heat alone thickens egg and cheese into a glossy cream that
  coats each strand — a splash of the saved pasta water loosens it if it
  tightens. The pan never goes back on the flame: direct heat is how
  carbonara becomes spaghetti with scrambled eggs.
</Step>

<Step id="insurance_gradual" title="Insurance: go gradual" when="insurance">
  With supermarket cheese, don't pour — alternate. A little egg-and-cheese
  mixture, a few drops of pasta water, stir; again, until it's all in.
  Gradual addition is what keeps an additive-laden cheese melting instead
  of seizing.
</Step>

<Step id="add_cream" title="The cream variation" when="has(cream, 'heavy_cream')">
  Stir in the <Ref of="cream" /> until the sauce turns pale and dependable.
  Silky every time, no nerve required — and, as the badge above now says,
  no longer the Academy's carbonara.
</Step>

<Step id="serve" title="Pepper and serve">
  Plate immediately and finish with the
  <Ref of="seasoning" fill="black_pepper" /> — coarse, fresh, more than
  feels polite. Carbonara waits for no one: the silk window is minutes
  long, so the table comes to the pasta.
</Step>

</ol>

### Tips

- The certified recipe is itself heretical by internet standards: the
  Accademia Italiana della Cucina's deposited version uses whole eggs, olive
  oil, and a browned-and-removed garlic clove. If a purist objects, the
  badge at the top of the page is on your side.
- The internet's "foolproof" carbonara methods — double boilers, temperature
  charts, cornstarch, blenders — are chef technique imported on top of a
  four-ingredient dish. They work; they're also workarounds for the two real
  failure points this page handles directly: heat (rest the pan) and cheese
  quality (buy DOP, or blend with Parmigiano-Reggiano).
- The cheese lesson, learned from Pasta Grammar: in Italy, even cheap
  pecorino melts properly; abroad, "pecorino" is a catch-all name and the
  additives in pre-grated cheese are exactly what makes sauces clump. Buy a
  DOP wedge, grate it fine, and the dish gets dramatically easier.
- If eating lightly cooked egg worries you, the serving-bowl method here
  keeps temperatures gentle — stir longer over the warm pasta and the sauce
  pasteurizes in practice; pregnant or immunocompromised cooks should use
  pasteurized eggs.

- 网上的「万无一失」卡邦尼拉——隔水加热、温度曲线、玉米淀粉、料理机——是把
  大厨技术叠在一道四样食材的菜上。有效，但真正的失败原因只有两个：温度
  （让锅歇一歇）和奶酪品质（买DOP，或掺帕玛森）。本页直接解决这两个。
- 奶酪的教训（来自 Pasta Grammar）：在意大利，便宜的佩科里诺也能化得顺滑；
  在国外，「pecorino」是个谁都能用的名字，预擦丝奶酪里的抗结剂正是酱汁结块的
  元凶。买整块DOP、自己擦细，这道菜会容易得多。
- 担心蛋未全熟：碗中拌和的做法温度很温和，在热面上多搅一会儿实际上就完成了
  巴氏消毒；孕期或免疫力低的人请用杀菌蛋。
